## The new cap, and what changes for a user

**Derived maximum: 57,216 → 52,360 characters.** The schema term over the full
catalogue (9 tools, 4,854 chars of JSON) is 1,214 estimated tokens; the real tokenizer
measured 1,052.

**The configured cap stays 50,000**, B6a's figure rounded down, and it still fits.
Headroom beside a maximal message falls from ~1,804 tokens to ~590.

**For a user, nothing changes today:** a message up to 50,000 characters is accepted as
before. Notes' two drafted tools (~369 estimated tokens) still fit. **The next tool
after Notes fails `test_tool_schemas_fit_the_headroom_beside_a_maximal_message`**, and
the cap then has to come down, to about 49,000 per extra ~250-token tool. That is when a
user would see a lower limit, on a 413.

## B21's order of giving up (as approved)

1. Older history, newest kept first. Decision #6's normal windowing: not marked, since
   what falls out stays retrievable.
2. The retrieved records: continuation pieces first (a smaller cap, B17's order), then
   whole hits from the lowest rank. The prompt says how many hits were left out.
   Event `records_shrunk`.
3. The oldest whole tool rounds, never half of one. Event `rounds_dropped`.
4. The last resort: no tools on this call, assembled again without the schemas. Event
   `final_call_forced`.
5. Nothing left to give up: the question goes over budget, as before. Event `overflow`.

## Tested

- **The reproduction's scenarios are the regression test.** Every case that dropped
  the question before now keeps it on every call:
  - a 50,000-char message after 3 calls;
  - after 2 rounds of 2;
  - ~9,500 chars at the records cap with 3 calls/round;
  - ~42,800 chars with 1 call/round;
  - ~41,700 chars at 25,000 chars of records.

  The script's sweep now reports *never dropped* at every point.
- **Order, on real retrieval results:**
  - moderate pressure withholds pieces and keeps all 10 hits;
  - heavy pressure keeps the top hits, says how many were left out, and the lowest is
    gone;
  - at 4 rounds of 5 calls the hits go 8 → 4 → 0 across iterations before any round is
    dropped. This is asserted, after a first version of that test passed by vacuity.
- Last resort: a fat schema at `num_ctx` 4,096 gets a call with no tools, and the
  answer still arrives.
- Overflow: a question larger than the window is still sent, and marked.
- Markers: the gate receives calls only, receipts ignore markers, and a markers-only
  trace has `called_tools` false.
- **Proven to bite:**
  - B20: a 3,000-char schema fails both of its tests, each naming its numbers;
  - B20: a 52,500 cap fails the derivation;
  - B21, seven mutations, each restored and checked byte-identical: no pinning fails
    12; rounds before records 3; hits before pieces 1; events not traced 7; the last
    resort ignored 1; `called_tools` counting markers 1; gate given the raw trace 1.
- **Full suite:** 1,436 passed, 4 skipped. `ruff` is clean.

## Known limitations

- **After a round is dropped, the model can call the same tool again**, since its
  earlier call is no longer in the prompt. Bounded by `max_iterations`, and visible in
  the trace.
- **Older-history windowing is unmarked**, on purpose (see 1 above).
- **A trace can now hold entries that are not calls**, on a turn with no tools at all
  (a `records_shrunk` with no rounds is not possible, but `overflow` is). Consumers of
  the stored `tool_trace` outside `program/` (diagnostic scripts, the Phase 9 frontend)
  should use `call_entries`.
- **Schemas are priced with the 4.0 estimator**: safe on these schemas (4.61 real), and
  re-measured when the catalogue changes shape.
