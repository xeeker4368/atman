# Piece 3.5 — lifecycle and concurrency (Tier 3)

Design of record: `docs/DESIGN_3.5_2026-10-04.md`, including the rulings and amendments at its top,
which win where the design (written before them) differs. Source plan: `docs/FIX_PLAN_2026-10-04.md`
§3.5. No migration: the schema is untouched, and versions 9 and 10 stay reserved (piece 7, piece 5).

Built in six commits: the design, the three reproductions as failing tests, then one commit per step.

## Step 0 — the reproductions, committed failing

`tests/test_lifecycle.py` holds the three reproductions from the design's section 0.1. Each was
committed **failing**, before any fix, so the defect is in the measurement of record rather than only
in a report. Run on the step-0 commit, `PYTHONDONTWRITEBYTECODE=1 venv/bin/python -m pytest -q
tests/test_lifecycle.py`: `3 failed in 0.73s`.

```
FAILED test_a_reply_saved_after_the_conversation_closed_is_indexed_or_refused
E   AssertionError: the reply was saved into a closed conversation and is in no chunk;
E                   in the recovery queue: False

FAILED test_a_rerun_after_a_failed_upsert_writes_the_missing_vector
E   AssertionError: 1 of 2 chunk rows still have no vector after a second run;
E                   chunking skipped them on their stored text

FAILED test_a_sweep_does_not_close_a_conversation_that_got_a_message_after_its_snapshot
E   AssertionError: the sweep closed a conversation that received a message after its snapshot
E   assert '2026-10-04T21:38:09.310493+00:00' is None
```

What each one shows:

1. `db.save_message` accepts a message for a conversation whose `ended_at` is set. The reply is then
   in no chunk — `checkpoint_conversation` never seals the open trailing group — and the conversation
   is **not** in `db.get_unchunked_ended_conversations()` either, because `chunked` is already 1. So
   nothing in the system will ever index it.
2. A failed vector upsert leaves a chunk row with no vector, and `chunking._write_group`'s skip path
   (`chunks_skipped += 1`) means every later run leaves it that way. Only
   `scripts/reconcile_vectors.py` repairs it, and nothing runs that.
3. `idle.close_idle_conversations` takes its candidates, then closes them, with no recheck. A message
   that arrives in between is chunked as the tail of a conversation that has just been closed under
   its own turn.

The design's section 0.2 also tested the obvious alternative to (1) — reopening the conversation —
and recorded why it is not available: clearing `ended_at` and re-finalising raises
`ChunkIntegrityError`, because adding a message changes the text of a chunk index that is already
stored.

## Step 1 — one turn at a time per conversation (Tier 2)

`program/engine/turn_locks.py` (new) holds a `threading.Lock` per conversation id. Its own module so
`idle.py` can ask `held(...)` without importing `turn.py`.

- A second turn in the same conversation is **refused with 409**, not queued: a turn is bounded at
  roughly 35 minutes, so holding the request could outlast answering it, and a refusal makes a client
  retry harmless. The body says how long the running turn has been going, which is the useful
  question for whoever is refused.
- The guard is taken **after** the conversation is resolved (it needs the id) and **before** anything
  is written: a refused send leaves no message, starts no conversation and makes no model call.
- A send with **no** `conversation_id` is never refused — the id does not exist until
  `db.start_conversation` returns.
- `handle_user_message`'s body moved into `_answer()` so the guard reads as one line rather than as an
  indented block around everything. No other behaviour changed.
- In-process only (one uvicorn worker), not a database lock. The cross-process backstop is step 2.

Tests: `tests/test_turn_guard.py`, 10 (two with real threads and a blocking model; the route's 409
proven with the lock held by the test, which needs no race).

**Mutations, each failing its own test:** blocking acquire; the 409 without the age; the guard moved
after the save; the `finally` removed; keyed by user instead of conversation; one global lock; the
route not passing `is_busy`.

## Step 2 — the close and the save are atomic (Tier 3)

- **`db.save_message` refuses an ended conversation**, raising `db.ConversationClosed` with nothing
  written. The guard is part of the archive insert (`INSERT … SELECT … WHERE NOT EXISTS (… ended_at IS
  NOT NULL)`) rather than a read before it, so the conversation cannot be closed between the check and
  the write. **The single-statement atomicity rests on `DELETE` journal mode**, which the docstring now
  says. A *missing* conversation is deliberately unchanged: the working-store insert fails its foreign
  key and the caller still gets `IntegrityError`.
- **`db.end_conversation(..., not_after=)` returns whether it closed**, and the sweep passes the
  snapshot it judged. A conversation that received a message since is left open, counted in
  `skipped_recently_active`, and **not chunked** — finalising it would seal its trailing group while it
  is still live. `find_idle_conversations` returns `IdleCandidate(conversation_id, reason,
  last_message_at)`.
- The in-process sweep also skips a conversation whose turn lock is held (`is_busy`, passed in by the
  route so `idle.py` keeps knowing nothing about turns).
- **`get_open_conversations_with_activity` picks the last message deterministically.** `role` was a
  bare column beside `MAX(timestamp)`, which SQLite takes from an arbitrary row of the group, so two
  messages in the same second could report either role — and the sweep would then choose either
  window. It now reads the role from the latest row by `timestamp`, with `rowid` as the tie-break.
- **A reply whose conversation closed mid-turn is saved into a new conversation, alone**, with
  `new_conversation` true and both ids in a WARNING. The question is already on record and chunked
  where it was asked; copying it would put a second archive row under one utterance. The cost is
  stated: the new conversation's first chunk is an answer with no question, and nothing links the two.
  If the close beats the *question*, the turn simply continues in a fresh conversation.

No schema change. Reproductions 1 and 3 pass from here.

Tests: 11 in `tests/test_lifecycle.py`. **Mutations:** the unconditional insert; `EXISTS (… IS NULL)`
instead of `NOT EXISTS (… IS NOT NULL)`; the `NOT EXISTS` clause dropped from the close; counting a
close that did not happen; the assistant-save handling removed; the user-save retry removed; the bare
column restored; `@retry_on_locked` removed from `save_message`.

## Step 3 — a missing vector is repaired, the recovery queue is drained (Tier 3)

- `chunking._write_group`'s skip path **upserts the vector when the row exists and the store does not
  hold one**, counted as `vectors_repaired` (separate from `vectors_indexed`: a non-zero repair count
  means an upsert is failing somewhere). Guarded by `store.indexes_vectors`, or a store that holds
  nothing would look as though every vector were missing. The text check still runs first.
- `checkpoint_conversation` **includes the open tail when `ended_at` is set**. Once ended no message
  can be added (step 2), so there is no open group to protect and the pass is idempotent.
- `idle.drain_recovery_queue(limit=1)` finishes final chunking for conversations closed but not
  chunked. `db.get_unchunked_ended_conversations` had existed since task 1.3 with nothing draining it.
  The chat route drains **one** after the response; `scripts/close_idle_conversations.py --drain
  [--limit N]` does it on demand. **Not at startup**: the drain embeds, and a slow or absent model
  must not delay serving (a deviation from the plan's "at startup and by script", approved as ruling 4).

`tests/test_chunking.py` wall time: **0.48–0.49 s before, 0.50 s after** (two runs each). A test
asserts the repair does not fire on an ordinary run, which it does not.

Reproduction 2 passes from here. Tests: 12 more in `tests/test_lifecycle.py`, 2 in
`tests/test_turn_guard.py`. **Mutations:** the bare `chunks_skipped += 1`; the `indexes_vectors` guard
dropped; the repair moved above the text check; `include_open_tail=False` when ended; a checkpoint
marking the conversation chunked; the drain removed from the route; an unbounded drain; the drain
raising out of the background task.

## Step 4 — a vector-store failure degrades the leg, and the embedding query is bounded (Tier 3)

- **B24.** `_vector_leg` guarded `ollama.embed` and not the `store.query` on the next line, so a Chroma
  error propagated out of `retrieval.search()` and the turn ran with **no** retrieved records while the
  lexical leg's results were available — observed in every failing turn of B23. The query is now
  guarded the same way, with the **same log phrase** (`vector leg unavailable`), so one leg being down
  reads the same way whichever half of it failed, and the kit's tripwire keeps marking the turn.
  Nothing new is said to the model: `memory_search`'s existing *"this search was incomplete"* sentence
  covers it, and the passive block says nothing about a downed leg, as it already did not.
- **Truncation.** The text sent to the embedder is at most `config.embedding_max_input_chars()`
  (5,000, read from config), a **prefix** (ruling 5). `ollama.embed` deliberately does not truncate,
  and a message may be 50,000 characters against a 2,048-token embedding context, so a long paste
  probably lost the vector leg on every turn it was the query. Logged at **INFO**, not WARNING: a long
  paste is routine and nothing is degraded, and a WARNING would mark the turn as degraded.
  **The limitation, recorded:** the cut can land mid-word, and what a long message says after the
  budget is not embedded. The stored message and the lexical leg are untouched.

Tests: 5 in `tests/test_retrieval.py`, 1 in `tests/test_memory_search.py`. **Mutations:**
`store.query` unguarded (fails the retrieval and the `memory_search` tests); the log phrase changed;
`except BaseException` (the isolation violation must still propagate); the truncation removed; the
limit hard-coded.

## Step 5 — B23: recovery for the loud shape, refusal for the quiet one (Tier 3)

**`flock` was proven first**, as the amendment required, in two real processes on both filesystems
this project uses: the data volume (`/dev/disk5s1`, APFS, `/Volumes/Dock Storage`, in a throwaway
directory beside the repository rather than in `data/`) and a scratch directory (`/dev/disk3s1`,
APFS). A live holder refuses a second process with `BlockingIOError` (errno 35); a holder killed with
`SIGKILL` releases it immediately, leaving the file and its now-meaningless text behind. Test fixtures
were also checked, not only test functions: **no fixture or test creates two `TestClient`s against one
store** — every lifespan is entered sequentially — so refusing a second server breaks nothing.

- **Recovery.** `ChromaVectorStore.query` catches Chroma's `InternalError`, clears Chroma's shared
  system cache, reopens this store's own client and collection, and retries **once**; a second failure
  propagates into step 4's degraded leg. **Both halves are needed, measured:** clearing the cache alone
  leaves this object holding the old collection, and reopening alone is handed the same cached system
  back. `clear_system_cache` is an internal Chroma API, which is why the pin matters and why a test
  fails loudly if it stops existing.
- **Refusal.** `program/ops/store_lock.py` takes an `flock` on `data_dir()/server.lock` — a file inside
  a directory that already has backup and isolation coverage, so no new config key and no new runtime
  directory. The server holds it for its lifetime, taken **after** the secret check and the capability
  probe (an unconfigured server still touches nothing) and **before** the databases are created. The
  four vector-writing scripts refuse while it is held: `close_idle_conversations`, `reconcile_vectors`,
  `seed_dataset`, `write_journal --index`. `backup.py`, `note.py` and `set_password.py` are not
  covered, and the enumeration test says why; readers are never refused. **No force flag** — the
  correct response is to stop the server.
- **`flock` rather than a pid file** because the kernel releases it when the holder dies, so there is
  no stale lock to detect or clear; `os.kill(pid, 0)` is wrong exactly when it matters, because a
  reused pid reads as alive. The file's text only makes the refusal legible.
- Locks held in-process are keyed by resolved lock path, not kept as one flag: a test process runs
  many stores in one interpreter, and a lock taken for one must say nothing about another.

**A correction to the design, found by building it.** The design said the recovery covers both shapes.
It covers both *when it runs*, but its **trigger only fires on the loud shape**: the quiet shape raises
nothing, so there is nothing to catch. Measured again here — with a vector already in the collection,
another process's write leaves `count()` and `has()` seeing it while `query()` never returns it. So the
**refusal is what closes the quiet shape**, and both facts are now checked properties
(`test_the_recovery_cures_a_stale_view_when_it_runs`, and
`test_a_stale_view_raises_nothing_which_is_why_a_script_is_refused`, which fails if Chroma ever starts
reporting it).

**Phase 6, stated rather than left to be discovered:** a launchd `write_journal --index` will be
refused by this guard. The smallest fix is a loopback-gated admin route that indexes one artifact id
inside the server's process, with the launchd job calling it; J7's control is unchanged because the
route takes an explicit id and never scans. Recorded in `NOW.md` (now `docs/BACKLOG.md`) under B23. **Not built here.**

Tests: `tests/test_store_lock.py`, 15, two of them with a real second process. **Mutations:** the cache
clear removed; the reopen removed; a retry loop instead of one retry; the script's guard removed; the
lifespan's lock removed; the probe moved before the secret check; and a throwaway decoy script that
writes vectors without the guard (the enumeration catches it).

## What this piece did not do

- **No migration.** The schema is untouched; versions 9 and 10 stay reserved for piece 7 and piece 5.
- **No prompt-facing text changed**, so no frozen gate or correction number can move. The pinned
  digests pass unchanged: the gate's byte-identity digest, the B20/B21 fitting-turn digest, the B17
  render, and the piece-2 turn digest.
- **The kit's `/kit/end` route is not edited** (it lives outside the repository). It should take the
  turn lock; the patch is in the task report. Unchanged, it is still covered by step 2: a reply whose
  conversation it closes mid-turn lands in a new conversation rather than being indexed by nothing.

## Adjacent problems, reported and not fixed

- **One intermittent failure of my own test**, seen once in the full suite and never again (the file
  passes repeatedly in isolation, and two subsequent full runs were clean). It was a threaded test
  that joined with a 10-second timeout and then read the thread's result, so a slow join surfaced as a
  missing key rather than as a diagnosis. One such test is now deterministic (the lock held by the
  test), and the other reports what its thread actually did. A turn in these tests takes about 15 ms,
  so the timeout was never the real bound.
- **`seed_dataset` takes the store lock before the store-version refusal** inside `seed.seed()`, so a
  stale store is refused a moment after the lock is taken rather than before. Harmless (the lock is
  released at exit and nothing is written), and left alone rather than restructuring the script.
- **The recovery leaks the old Chroma system.** `clear_system_cache()` empties two dicts without
  stopping the systems they held, so a recovery may leave the previous reader to be collected rather
  than closed. Unmeasured, and the recovery is rare by design.
